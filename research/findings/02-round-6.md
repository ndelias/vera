# Round 6: product engineers and PMs, in their own words

1,066 Hacker News posts where people name the role, theirs or as a topic.
I read 40 where product engineers speak and 40 where PMs speak. Nahuel
hasn't reviewed them yet.

## "Product engineer" is a hiring term

28 of the 40 product-engineer posts are job ads or people looking for
work. Only 3 describe a problem. On public forums, product engineers don't
write about their work as product engineers. They write as engineers,
which the HN rounds already cover.

That isn't a dead end. It's a market signal: lots of companies are hiring
for the role (Ashby, PostHog, GitBook, Langfuse, YC and many more in this
sample alone). It fits Stage 2's "is there a buyer" question.

## PMs are starting to build

15 of the 40 PM posts are relevant (38%). The theme nobody had predicted:
**PMs are shipping code themselves with AI.**

- A PM whose PRs are reviewed by a senior engineer before merging. Most
  get merged without much comment, and "his bar is high".
- PMs and technical operators taking over small integrations, scripts and
  internal tools that used to need an engineer.
- PMs building their own data jobs instead of waiting on someone.
- GitHub PMs on the other side of it: maintainers flooded with
  low-quality PRs, and GitHub building ways to restrict who can open them.
- Matrix management: a PM needing approvals from so many cross-functional
  counterparts that "we could barely get anything done".

## What this does to the candidates

The Blast Radius premise (non-engineers making code changes) shows up
here, but as **a trend that's already happening**, not as a complaint
about waiting on engineering. Nobody asks for permission to ship. They
just do it with AI. The open question is **what happens at review**: who
checks a PM's or an agent's change, how much they trust it, and how many
approvals it needs.

That's where Blast Radius and the review-and-approval candidate meet.
Both point at the same place: **review is where changes from AI and
non-engineers pile up.** Still a hypothesis (H7, next to H6 and G1–G3).
